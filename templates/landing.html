Step 1: Student Record Ingestion & Format Validation

Automatic

Colleges upload student batches via CSV or single-entry forms. The system validates the APAAR identifier against standard rule ^\d{12}$ and automatically masks it for privacy (e.g. XXXXXXXX9012).

Sample Record: Aarav Sharma • Programme: Computer Engineering • APAAR: XXXXXXXX9012 • Format Valid
{# Step 2 Content #}

Step 2: Dynamic Seat Ledger Intake Control

Prevent Pillar

Every admission checks available capacity. In a 120-seat programme, admission #120 succeeds. If a college attempts to submit admission #121, the transaction is rejected immediately with CAPACITY_EXCEEDED to stop ghost admissions.

Ledger Rule: Regular seats (120/120) are strictly separate from Supernumerary quotas (EWS/TFWS). Over-admissions are logged into the regulatory audit trail.
{# Step 3 Content #}

Step 3: Identity Graph & Risk Resolution

Detect Pillar

The system links student records across institutions. If a student is simultaneously enrolled at two institutions (e.g. VESIT Mumbai and COEP Pune), a case is opened with transparent risk point weights (Duplicate APAAR +40, Simultaneous Enrolment +35).

Human in the Loop: No student is ever cancelled automatically. Regulators review the evidence chain to confirm data errors or genuine dual enrolments.
{# Step 4 Content #}

Step 4: Academic Passport & QR Proof

Prove Pillar

Upon completion, degrees are signed using Ed25519 asymmetric cryptography. Employers and verifiers can scan a QR code to verify validity in milliseconds without needing database credentials or exposing student PII.

Offline Capable: Standard public key cryptography enables offline verification with selective consent sharing (24h/72h/168h expiry).
